5. Content block

Insurance 101 · Umbrella

What an umbrella policy actually covers

Desert Peak Insurance · Reviewed for Arizona, Nevada, Utah and Idaho

An umbrella policy sits above your auto and home liability limits. It does not replace them, and it will not pay a dollar until the policy beneath it has paid its full limit. That is the whole design: cheap, high-limit coverage that only responds to the claims large enough to exhaust everything else.

Most carriers require underlying limits of $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident on auto, and $300,000 on home, before they will write a $1,000,000 umbrella. If your current auto policy carries state minimums, the umbrella quote will come with a condition: raise the underlying limits first. The Arizona minimums are here.

When it is not worth buying

If you rent, own no vehicle and have no assets a judgement could reach, an umbrella policy is protecting something you do not have. We will tell you that. The analysis costs nothing, and if keeping what you have is the right answer, that is the answer you get.
