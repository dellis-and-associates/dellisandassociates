Desert Peak Insurance — Brand System

Desert Peak Insurance brand and design system — typography, color, spacing, components, motion, editorial voice, and insurance/Medicare compliance guardrails. Load before building or editing any page, component, or marketing copy for desertpeakinsurance.com.

Standing reference for every page, component and line of copy on desertpeakinsurance.com. Load this before writing anything. Where a task brief and this document disagree on tokens, this document wins — task briefs describe work, this describes the brand.

1. The business, in one paragraph

Desert Peak Insurance is an independent agency licensed in Arizona, Nevada, Utah and Idaho, appointed with 40+ carriers, writing personal lines (auto, home, renters, umbrella, motorcycle, landlord), life and annuities, health and Medicare, and commercial (GL, BOP, commercial auto, workers' comp, E&O, cyber). It is a rebrand of Ellis & Associates. Principal advisor: Daniel Ellis. The core offer is a free, no-obligation policy analysis: send a declarations page, a licensed advisor compares it line by line against the carriers the agency represents, and returns a written finding with the numbers side by side.

The proposition is stated in the homepage H1 and governs everything else:

Compared across carriers. Explained with the math.

The differentiator is not price. It is willingness to tell you nothing needs to change. Every page must preserve that.

2. Voice

Plain, numeric, unflattering. The agency sounds like a competent advisor explaining a document across a table — not like marketing.

Do

  • Second person. "What you own, owe and earn."
  • Concrete arithmetic over adjectives. A worked example with real figures beats any claim of expertise.
  • State the limit of the advice. "It is not everyone. A renter with no car, no savings and no dependants has little a judgment could take."
  • Short declaratives. Let a sentence end.
  • Name the mechanism: reconstruction estimator, special enrollment period, interested party, additional insured. The audience can handle the real term if you define it in the same breath.

Don't

  • Superlatives, urgency, or enthusiasm. No "best," "trusted," "peace of mind," "we've got you covered."
  • Rhetorical questions as headings.
  • Exclamation marks, emoji, ALL CAPS for emphasis.
  • Padding: "In today's world," "when it comes to," "it's important to note that."
  • Promise an outcome. The analysis produces a finding, not a saving.

House style

  • No Oxford comma. "Arizona, Nevada, Utah and Idaho."
  • "advisor," not "adviser." "dependant," not "dependent," for a person. (Note: dependant is the British form and is unusual for a US audience — it is the current site's convention, so stay consistent, but flag it if the client is open to switching.)
  • Spell out numbers under one hundred in prose ("twenty minutes"); use figures for money, limits, percentages and phone numbers ("$350,000," "40+ carriers," "801-300-9980").
  • States spelled out in body copy; two-letter codes only in tight UI (tags, nav, meta).
  • Sentence case for all headings. Never title case.
  • "Medicare" is always capitalised. "Part B," "Part D." Never "medicare."

Instrument Serif rules. It is a high-contrast display face and it fails when misused:

  • Never below 28px. Its thin strokes vanish at text sizes — a real problem for a Medicare audience.
  • Never for body copy, UI controls, form labels, nav items, table cells, or any string under ~10 words.
  • Always negative tracking, tighter as size grows.
  • font-optical-sizing: auto and text-wrap: balance on every display heading.
  • Control line breaks manually on hero headlines rather than letting the viewport decide.

DM Mono rules.

  • Eyebrows: uppercase, 0.1em tracking, --ink-muted, 12px, sitting 32–40px above the heading it labels.
  • All figures: font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so columns align.
  • Never for sentences. Labels, indices and numbers only.

Font loading. Self-hosted via next/font, display: swap, Latin subset, adjustFontFallback or explicit size-adjust on the fallback so the swap causes no shift.

4. Color, surface, line

Derive from the existing Strata brand tokens in the repo. Read them before writing any color; add only what is missing. Map onto these semantic roles:

--surface-base      page background — warm off-white, never pure #fff
--surface-raised    cards and panels — a 2–4% step from base
--surface-inverse   the single dark band per page
--ink-primary       headings and body — target 13:1 on base
--ink-secondary     lede and supporting copy — ≥ 7:1
--ink-muted         eyebrows, captions, metadata — ≥ 4.5:1
--line-hairline     1px dividers and borders, ~8–12% ink
--line-strong       hover and active borders, ~24% ink
--accent            links, focus rings, active states
--accent-ink        text color meeting AA on --accent

Discipline. Accent occupies under 5% of the pixels on any page. Authority in this category comes from neutral hierarchy and exact spacing, not from color.

Depth comes from hairlines and surface steps, never shadows. box-shadow is banned on static elements; permitted only as a low-opacity hover affordance.

5. Space and layout

--band-pad-y: clamp(5rem, 9vw, 10rem);
--band-gap:   clamp(2.5rem, 4vw, 4rem);
--shell-max:  1280px;
--gutter:     clamp(1.25rem, 4vw, 2.5rem);
--measure:    64ch;   /* prose max-width — never exceed 68ch */
  • 12-column grid. Prefer asymmetric splits (7/5, 5/7) over centered symmetry for hero and feature bands.
  • Every major band separated by a full-shell-width 1px --line-hairline. Consistent hairlines at consistent intervals are what make a page read as engineered rather than assembled.
  • Breakpoints to verify: 320, 360, 768, 1024, 1440, 1920. No horizontal scroll at any.

6. Components

Buttons. Two only. Primary: filled --accent, --accent-ink, Figtree 500, 44px min height, radius from the Strata tokens. Secondary: transparent with --line-strong border. No third variant, no icon-only CTAs, no gradient fills. Hover transitions border and ink over 150ms — no scale, no lift.

Cards. --surface-raised, 1px --line-hairline, no shadow. Internal padding ≥ 24px mobile / 32px desktop. Structure: mono eyebrow → Figtree H3 → body → link. Whole card clickable only if it has a single destination.

Link lists (line groups, state lists, glossary indexes). These carry the site's internal-linking weight — treat them as primary UI. Body size, ≥ 12px vertical padding per item, hairline between items, underline on hover.

Accordions. Real <button> + aria-expanded, content in the DOM, keyboard operable. Question in Figtree 500, answer at body size on a 68ch measure. First item open by default.

Forms. Labels always visible above the field — never placeholder-only. 17px input text (prevents iOS zoom). Errors in text next to the field, never color alone. Mono for field hints.

Tables (plan and coverage comparisons). Figtree body, mono tabular figures, hairline row rules, no zebra striping. Horizontally scrollable on mobile inside a labelled region, never squeezed.

Stats. Instrument Serif or DM Mono numeral at --fs-stat, mono caption beneath. Never a donut chart, never an animated counter.

7. Motion

--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1);
--dur-enter: 520ms;
--dur-hover: 150ms;
  • Entrance only: opacity 0→1 + translateY(12px→0) via IntersectionObserver, once per element, rootMargin: "0px 0px -12% 0px".
  • Stagger siblings 60ms, capped at 5.
  • Never animate the LCP heading. It paints immediately.
  • Everything behind @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference); reduced-motion users get the final state with identical layout.
  • Banned: parallax, scroll-jacking, counting numbers, typewriter effects, cursor followers, autoplay carousels.

8. Page-type patterns

Insurance line page (36 of these — consistency matters more than invention) Mono eyebrow (category) → H1 (line name) → lede defining the coverage in one sentence → what it covers / what it does not, as parallel lists → a worked example with real figures → who needs it and who does not → how the analysis handles this line → related lines → FAQ → CTA.

State page (AZ, NV, UT, ID) H1 naming the state → what is legally required there, with the actual minimums → what the minimums fail to cover → local risk factors → lines written in that state → cities served → licensing disclosure link → CTA.

City page Thin pages are a liability. Every city page needs something true and specific to that city, or it should not exist. No spun paragraphs with the name swapped.

Glossary term Term as H1 → one-sentence plain definition → why it matters to a policyholder → an example → related terms. Definition-first, always.

Resource/guide Editorial. Instrument Serif H1, 64ch measure, mono metadata line (date, reviewer), real subheadings. Show the licensed reviewer and the review date — the editorial policy page exists to back this up.

Quote flow Progress indicator, one question group per step, everything resumable. Say what happens to the data before asking for it. No dark patterns, no pre-checked consent, no forced phone field before value is delivered.

9. Compliance guardrails — hard

Insurance and Medicare marketing is regulated. These are enforcement rules, not preferences. The agency's own compliance review is the final authority; when unsure, flag rather than publish.

  • Never "best," "cheapest," "lowest rates," "guaranteed savings," or any promised outcome.
  • Never state a premium, rate or saving without noting it varies by carrier, state and underwriting.
  • Never imply government affiliation or endorsement. No Medicare, CMS, SSA or state-agency logos, seals or lookalike marks.
  • Never claim to offer every Medicare plan. Medicare-related pages carry the existing plan-availability disclaimer verbatim.
  • Never imply the site binds coverage. The "independent agency, not an insurer" line stays.
  • Never imply service outside AZ, NV, UT, ID.
  • Carrier names are trademarks. Text only, unless licensed logo assets already exist in the repo. Never imply a carrier endorses the agency.
  • Awards carry their date and scope. The Circle of Champions framing — a personal recognition of a named producer for one plan year, and dated — is the model for every credential on the site.
  • Never fabricate a statistic, testimonial, review score, carrier count or years-in-business figure. If a number is not sourced, it does not ship.
  • Footer disclosures (Medicare, licensing, not-an-insurer) are frozen in wording and placement. Restyle for legibility only — never shrink, mute or collapse them behind an interaction.

10. Accessibility floor — hard

This audience skews older. Medicare is a core line.

  • WCAG 2.2 AA minimum; target AAA (7:1) for body text.
  • Body copy floor 17px everywhere, footer and disclosures included.
  • Interactive targets ≥ 44×44px with a visible :focus-visible ring. Never remove an outline without a stronger replacement.
  • One <h1> per page, gapless heading order.
  • Never color alone to convey meaning.
  • All imagery has real alt text; decorative images get alt="".
  • Full keyboard traversal on every interactive element.

11. Anti-patterns

Never appear on this site: stock handshakes, families on beaches, umbrella-and-shield clipart, gradient blobs, glassmorphism, countdown timers, "limited spots" banners, exit-intent modals, autoplay video heroes, chat-bubble popups, trust-badge soup, testimonial carousels on autoplay, marquee tickers, emoji in UI, "Get your FREE quote!!"

12. Before shipping

  • Three fonts only, each in its assigned role; Instrument Serif nowhere below 28px and in no paragraph or control.
  • No text below 17px.
  • Body contrast ≥ 7:1; all text ≥ 4.5:1 (check inverse bands explicitly).
  • Zero box-shadow on static elements.
  • Reduced-motion yields a static page with identical layout.
  • One <h1>, valid heading order, JSON-LD intact.
  • Every compliance rule in §9 verified against the page's actual copy.
  • No fabricated figures.
  • No horizontal scroll at 320–1920.
  • Content resolves through Payload; nothing hardcoded that should be CMS-driven.
  • Lighthouse mobile: Performance ≥ 90, Accessibility 100, SEO 100.
